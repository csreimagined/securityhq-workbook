User guide

How to fill in the value workbook

A short walkthrough for whoever's running the conversation — account manager, sales engineer or CISO — on what to enter on each page, what to just read aloud, and how the maths underneath it works.

00

Who should be in the room

The workbook is designed to be filled in live, on a call or in a workshop — not emailed over as a blank template. Get three people involved before you start page 4:

Security sponsorOwns the alert, incident and SOC numbers — CISO, security manager or IT lead.
Finance partnerConfirms hourly rates, downtime cost and what "value" means to the board.
Operational ownerKnows what critical systems and availability actually look like day to day.
01

Cover

Fill in

Enter the customer name, executive sponsor and date. The customer name isn't just decoration — type it once here and it carries through automatically to the headline, the board narrative on page 6, and the "for every £1 invested…" line, so you don't have to repeat yourself.

02

Value map & translation matrix

Reference — nothing to fill in

Pages 2 and 3 are talking points, not forms. Use the value map to frame the conversation around the customer's actual pain rather than SecurityHQ's feature list, and use the translation matrix as a script: capability → operational change → business outcome. Skim these live rather than reading them verbatim.

03

Baseline worksheet

Fill in

This is where you capture the customer's current state — before any calculation happens. It's split into three groups:

  • Operating profile — scope, critical systems, current SOC model, and current annual security spend. Mostly context; the spend figure is useful later for sanity-checking the technology-cost row on page 5.
  • Alert & response baseline — alert volume, false-positive rate, triage time and loaded hourly cost. These feed directly into the "analyst time released" calculation.
  • Incident & downtime baseline — incident history, downtime hours and audit prep effort. These set the "before" picture that page 5 measures improvement against.

Use a range and mark the confidence level (High / Medium / Low) rather than leaving a field blank — a defensible estimate beats a gap when this goes in front of a board.

04

ROSI calculator

Fill in — this is the core page

Six value components, each with its own inputs. Every field recalculates live — the total, ROSI and payback in the corner tray update as you type, so you can move numbers around in real time with the customer watching.

Analyst time releasedAlerts avoided/yr, minutes saved per alert, loaded £/houralerts × minutes ÷ 60 × £/hr

Growth enablementIncremental gross profit attributable to security assuranceentered directly
Once every row has a value, the worksheet totals them and works out: ROSI = (total annual value − annual investment) ÷ annual investment × 100 Payback (months) = annual investment ÷ total annual value × 12

Three rules to keep the number defensible:

  • Be conservative. When in doubt, round down. A credible £400k beats an inflated £1.2m.
  • Don't double-count. A downtime saving belongs in one row only — don't also fold it into incident loss avoided.
  • Write down the source. Use the "Assumption / source" column on the right of each row — "SOC time-and-motion study", "finance-agreed hourly rate" — so the number survives being challenged later.

What "annual SecurityHQ investment" means

This is the customer's all-in annual cost for the service — not a one-off. It's the denominator for both ROSI and payback, so get it from the actual proposal or contract rather than estimating it.

05

QBR scorecard

Fill in quarterlyNarrative auto-fills

This page is built for reuse — come back to it every quarter rather than filling it in once. The executive narrative at the top rewrites itself from whatever total value and payback you entered on page 5, so re-check it after you update the numbers.

The six-row scorecard tracks baseline vs. current for risk, response time, analyst efficiency, downtime, remediation backlog and realised commercial value. The last row compares realised value against your target automatically and flags on-track vs. behind.

06

Where your data goes

Everything you type is saved to localStorage in the browser you're using — nothing is sent to a server. That means:

  • Closing the tab and coming back later keeps your entries, on that same device and browser.
  • It won't follow you to a different laptop or browser — if you need to hand this to a colleague, use Print / PDF to export a filled-in copy first.
  • Reset in the top bar wipes every field on this device. There's no undo, so export first if you need a record.
